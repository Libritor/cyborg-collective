About

Alexander Vicol, University of Toronto (MEng ECE, MannLab Canada), with Steve Mann. This page is the public face of a year of wearable brain sensing on consumer hardware: the Muse headband, a phone that records it, and an XR headset that uses it. The idea behind it is Mann's Cyborg Collective: people who sense themselves, and share on their own terms.

Three rules hold everywhere on this page. Every number carries the contact quality it was measured under, and when the electrodes were not in contact the page says so instead of guessing. The activation estimate is a band-power ratio against the person's own baseline; it is not a measure of emotion, stress or health and is never called one. Nothing leaves a phone unless its owner opened the window, and what left is receipted on that phone.

Code: eyecam (the eye as a camera on a headset, public), HealthLogProtocol (consented health records with provenance, public); MuseLog and Cocaine Buzzkill on request.

How this page is honest
  • Live: when fewer than 3 of 4 electrodes are in contact the app sends no claim, and that is what is shown.
  • The record: an epoch counts as clean when its 2-second peak-to-peak swing stays under 200 µV; sessions from app versions that did not stream contact quality are marked so.
  • The relay keeps nothing; the record holds summaries only. Raw recordings stay with the person who made them.
