A window the person opens

Steve Mann's head, from his own CT scan

The skin is as the scanner found it; each fine line round it is one of the scan's 63 slices, three millimetres apart. Drag to turn the head. Pull the slider to open it: what shows on the cut is the scan itself at that height, bone white, the brain's folds and fluid inside.

The four dots are about where a Muse headband's electrodes rest. When someone shares their headband live, the dots take the colour of each contact and the brain in the opening ripples in the colours of that person's five bands. When nobody is, the one electrode the band's recorded minute came from keeps time with that minute.

Shown with Prof. Mann's agreement. Here of the scan: the skin as one surface and a small grey copy of the slices; no scanner file, and nothing from the scan's headers (no name, no dates). The idea of a head you can open is Rich Fletcher's, who put his own MRI on his page at MIT.
